# Capitalize the First Word of a Sentence:

Always capitalize the first word of a sentence:

> The cat is sleeping
> Where did I put that book?
> Hey! It's great to see you! How are you been?

# Capitalize the First Word a Quote (Sometimes):

Capitalize the first word of a quote when the quote is a complete sentence:

> Mario asked, **"What is everyone doing this weekend?"**
> Stacy answered, **"My sister and I are going to the water park"**

**DON'T CAPITALIZE THE FIRST WORD OF PARTIAL QUOTES:**

> Gretohen said she was "way to busy" the join the gym.
> Mr. Tomphson described the rules as "extremely difficult to understand" if you don't have a law degree.

# Don't capitalize seasons

The names of seasons, however, are not proper nouns. So there's no need to capitalize them.

> spring -> summer -> autumn -> winter

> Park Lane, Central Avenue, Pall Mall, George Street, Sydney Opera House, Central Park, Hyde Park, the Empire State Building, Wall Street.

# Capitalize Time Periods and Events (Sometimes)

Specific periods, eras, and historical events that have proper names should be capitalized.

> Most of the World War I veterans are now deceased.
> In the Middle Ages, poor hygiene was partly responsible for the spreading of bubonic plague.
> Middle school students often enjoy studying the social changes that took place during the Roaring Twenties.

However, centuries and the number before them - are not capitalized.

> In the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, England blossomed into an empire.

## Part 1. First Words & Quotes (1-3)

**1.** Which sentence is correctly capitalized?  
a) the cat is sleeping.  
b) The cat is sleeping.  
c) the Cat is sleeping.
> [!NOTE]- Question 1
> **b) The cat is sleeping.**  
> *Always capitalize the first word of a sentence.*

a) Mario asked, **"what is everyone doing this weekend?"**

c) Mario asked, **"What is everyone doing this weekend"?**
> [!NOTE]- Question 2
> **b) Mario asked, **"What is everyone doing this weekend?"**  
> *A full-sentence quote starts with a capital letter. The punctuation goes inside the quotation marks.*

**3.** Which partial quote is correctly capitalized?  
a) Gretchen said she was "Way too busy" to join the gym.  
b) Gretchen said she was "way too busy" to join the gym.  
c) Gretchen said she was "way too busy" To join the gym.
> [!NOTE]- Question 3
> **b) Gretchen said she was "way too busy" to join the gym.**  
> *Partial quotes (not a complete sentence) do not start with a capital letter.*
