# Countable nouns:

Countable nouns are for things we can count.
**They usually have a _singular and plural form_.**

examples:

```text
dog, two dogs, horse, ten horses, a man, six men, shop, the shops, a few ideas, idea...
```

**We can use the idefinite article _"a" / "an"_ with countable nouns:**

> **A** dog is **an** animal.

**When a countable noun is _singular_, we must a word like _"a" / "the" / "my" / "this"_ with it:**

> I want **an** orange. (~~Not I want orange~~).
> Where is **my** bottle? (~~Not Where is bottle?~~)

**When a countable noun is plural, we can use it alone:**

> I like oranges.
> Bottles can break.

**We can use _"some"_ and _"any"_ with countable nouns:**

> I've got **some** dollars
> Have you got **any** pens?

**We can use _"a few"_ and _"many"_ with countable nouns:**

> I've got **a few** dollars.
> I haven't got **many** pens.

## Uncountable nouns

### Uncountable nouns are for the things that we cannot count:

examples:

> tea, sugar, water, air, rice

**They are often the names for abstract ideas qualities:**

examples:

> knowledge, beauty, anger, fear, love

**They are used with a singular verb. They usually do not have the plural form. We cannot say ~~sugars~~, ~~angers~~, ~~knowledges~~.**

examples of common uncountable nouns:

> money, furniture, happiness, sadness, research, evidence, safety, beaty, knowledge.

**We cannot use _"a" / "an"_ with these nouns. To express to quantity of one on these nouns, use a word or expression like:**

> some, a lot of, a piece of, a bit of, a great deal of...

examples:

> There has been **a lot of**  _research_ into the causes of this disease
> He gave me **a great deal of** _advice_ before me interview.
> They've got **a lot of** _furniture_.
> Can you give me **some** _information_ about uncountable nouns?

**Some nouns are countable in others languages but uncountable in English. Some of the must common of these are:**

> accommodation
> advice
> baggage
> behavior
> bread
> furniture
> information
> luggage
> news
> progress
> traffic
> travel
> trouble
> weather
> work

#### BE CAREFUL:

**Sometimes the same noun can be countable and uncountable, often with a change of meaning.**

example: with the noun "hair" which is normally **uncountable** in English:

> She has long blonde hair

**It can also be _countable_ when reffering to individual hairs:**

> My father's getting a few grey hairs now

## 📌 Uncountable Nouns: Essential List
### 🧠 Abstract Ideas / Feelings
- **advice** → a piece of advice
- **information** → a piece of information
- **knowledge**
- **news** — *The news **is**...*
- **progress**
- **research**
- **evidence**
- **work** — *I need **work**.*
- **trouble**
- **behavior**
### 🪑 Groups & Materials
- **furniture**  — *a piece of furniture*
- **luggage** / **baggage**
- **traffic**
- **money** — *Much money, **a lot of** money.*
- **hair** — *She has long hair.*
- **bread** — *a loaf of bread*
- **paper** — *some paper*
- **wood**
### 💧 Nature & Substances
- **weather**
- water, air, rice, sugar, salt, sand, milk, tea, coffee
### ⚠️ Tricky Words

| Uncountable    | Countable      |
| :------------- | :------------- |
| **room**       | a room         |
| **time**       | a time / times |
| **experience** | an experience  |
| **glass**      | a glass        |
| **paper**      | a paper        |
| **hair**       | a hair         |
| **chicken**    | a chicken      |
| **noise**      | a noise        |
